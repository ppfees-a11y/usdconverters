Understand exchange rates before converting

A practical guide to using exchange-rate quotes without confusing the market rate with the amount you actually receive.

What “foreign currency units per USD” means

If a quoted USD/EUR rate is 0.92, one US dollar corresponds to 0.92 euros at that quoted rate. Multiply USD by 0.92 to estimate EUR. To convert EUR back to USD, divide by 0.92. The reverse rate is 1 ÷ 0.92, or approximately 1.08696 USD per EUR. These are illustrations, not current quotes.

Why a provider quote differs from a market quote

A published reference or mid-market rate is often not an executable rate for retail customers. Providers can earn revenue through an exchange-rate spread, a fixed fee, a percentage fee, or several of these at once. Card networks and issuing banks can also apply different rules to foreign purchases.

For meaningful comparisons, record the date and time of the quote, the source amount, the recipient currency, the rate offered, and the final amount delivered. Check for minimum charges and fees imposed by intermediary banks.

How to compare providers fairly

  1. Choose the same source amount and recipient currency for both quotes.
  2. Obtain both quotes close together in time; exchange rates move.
  3. Check the amount delivered after all fees, not just the headline rate.
  4. Review speed, refund terms, limits, and transfer eligibility before choosing.

Rates can move

Foreign-exchange rates change as market conditions evolve. This site deliberately does not prefill or imply a live quote. If you use a rate saved earlier, label it as historical and refresh it before a real transaction.

Understanding the true cost of a currency conversion

The headline exchange rate is only one part of the transaction. A provider may advertise a small transfer fee but build a markup into the conversion rate. Another may offer a rate close to a reference rate and charge an explicit fee. Compare the net amount delivered rather than relying on a single advertised percentage.

Reference rate versus customer rate

A mid-market rate is a reference midpoint between market buying and selling prices. Retail exchange services can offer a different customer rate. If a reference rate is 0.90 EUR for each USD but a provider offers 0.87 EUR, then $1,000 produces €870 rather than €900 before any separate fee. That is a €30 difference; it is not proof of any particular provider's current markup.

Know which currency a fee is charged in

A $5 source-currency fee is not the same as a €5 recipient-currency fee. A source fee changes the amount converted; a recipient fee is deducted after conversion. If the fee order is unclear, ask the provider for the final recipient amount and the full fee breakdown. Our tool models fixed and percentage charges on the source amount before conversion, and labels this limitation.

Travel, online purchases, and international transfers

For travel cash, compare the posted buy/sell rates and any service charge. For card purchases, check your card issuer's foreign transaction fee and whether the merchant offers to charge you in your home currency. For international transfers, confirm the amount the recipient will receive, the transfer speed, intermediary-bank charges, and any minimum fee. The lowest advertised fee is not necessarily the lowest total cost.

Five questions worth asking before you pay

  1. Is this the rate I will actually receive or a market reference?
  2. When does this rate expire?
  3. Are charges taken before or after conversion?
  4. Could intermediary or recipient fees reduce the final amount?
  5. What exact amount will arrive in the recipient's currency?

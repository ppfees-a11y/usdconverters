Currency conversion guide for U.S. travelers and businesses

1. Identify the base and quote currencies

USD/EUR means the number of euros per one U.S. dollar. Reversing the pair changes the rate to its reciprocal. A 0.90 USD-to-EUR rate means $100 converts to €90 before fees.

2. Use the rate date

Published reference rates typically reflect business-day observations, not a live transaction. Check the date shown on every calculation.

3. Compare total received

When sending money overseas, the actual amount received matters more than the advertised fee alone. Consider a provider's exchange spread, fixed charges, and payment method.

4. Budget for uncertainty

For future payments, test several hypothetical rates. A currency movement can change the U.S. dollar cost of a foreign invoice even if the invoice amount remains fixed.

5. Keep records

Save the currency pair, observation date, source, and the actual bank quote when preparing business expense records. A reference conversion is not proof of a settled transaction.
